Understand the idea

Business rules can live in application code, SQL statements, constraints, or stored routines. The right boundary depends on correctness, reuse, transaction needs, and team ownership. Set-based SQL can make a rule both more direct and more efficient when it applies to many rows. Start with the result the application needs or the rule the database must protect. Choose a PostgreSQL feature that expresses that requirement, then identify evidence that would show it is correct.

Small practice

Use a disposable schema or safe sample data to apply Business Logic. Predict the outcome, then check one boundary case and explain what PostgreSQL guarantees.

Practice

Choose a business invariant such as nonnegative stock. Decide where to enforce it and show how a database constraint protects every writer.

Do not infer performance from a tiny table or correctness from one happy-path row. Choose evidence that exposes the behavior you are learning.
